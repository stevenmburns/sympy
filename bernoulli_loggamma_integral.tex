\title{Evaluation of $\displaystyle I = \int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\Gamma(x)\,dx$}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

The second Bernoulli polynomial is $B_2(x) = x^2 - x + \tfrac{1}{6}$.
We show that
\[
  I = \int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\Gamma(x)\,dx = \frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi^2}.
\]

\section{Step 1: Reflection Formula}

The key observation is that $B_2$ is symmetric under $x\mapsto 1-x$:
\[
  B_2(1-x) = (1-x)^2-(1-x)+\tfrac{1}{6} = x^2-x+\tfrac{1}{6} = B_2(x).
\]
Substituting $x\mapsto 1-x$ in $I$ therefore gives
\[
  I = \int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\Gamma(1-x)\,dx.
\]
Adding the two expressions:
\[
  2I = \int_0^1 B_2(x)\bigl[\ln\Gamma(x)+\ln\Gamma(1-x)\bigr]\,dx.
\]
The reflection formula $\Gamma(x)\Gamma(1-x) = \pi/\sin(\pi x)$ gives
$\ln\Gamma(x)+\ln\Gamma(1-x) = \ln\pi - \ln\sin(\pi x)$, so
\[
  2I = \ln\pi\underbrace{\int_0^1 B_2(x)\,dx}_{=\,0}
       \;-\;\int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\sin(\pi x)\,dx,
\]
where the first integral vanishes because $\int_0^1 B_n(x)\,dx = 0$ for all $n\ge 1$.  Hence
\[
  I = -\frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\sin(\pi x)\,dx.
\]

\section{Step 2: Fourier Series of Both Factors}

The Fourier series of $\ln\sin(\pi x)$ on $(0,1)$ is the classical result
\[
  \ln\sin(\pi x) = -\ln 2 - \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\cos 2\pi kx}{k}.
\]
The Fourier series of the Bernoulli polynomial $B_2(x)$ on $[0,1]$ is
\[
  B_2(x) = \frac{1}{\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\cos 2\pi kx}{k^2}.
\]
(Verified at $x=0$: $\frac{1}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{\pi^2}{6} = \frac{1}{6} = B_2(0)$.)

\section{Step 3: Orthogonality Gives $\zeta(3)$}

Substituting into $I$, the $-\ln 2$ term drops out (again because $\int_0^1 B_2 = 0$):
\[
  I = -\frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\sin(\pi x)\,dx
    = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{1}{k}
      \int_0^1 B_2(x)\cos 2\pi kx\,dx.
\]
Inserting the Fourier series of $B_2$ and applying orthogonality
$\int_0^1\cos 2\pi jx\cos 2\pi kx\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}\delta_{jk}$:
\[
  \int_0^1 B_2(x)\cos 2\pi kx\,dx
  = \frac{1}{\pi^2}\cdot\frac{1}{k^2}\cdot\frac{1}{2}
  = \frac{1}{2\pi^2 k^2}.
\]
Therefore
\[
  I = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{2\pi^2 k^2}
    = \frac{1}{4\pi^2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{1}{k^3}
    = \frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi^2}.
\]

\section{Numerical Verification}

All three forms were verified to machine precision using \texttt{scipy.integrate.quad}:
\begin{align*}
  \int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\Gamma(x)\,dx &\approx 0.030\,448\,457\,058\,393,\\
  -\tfrac{1}{2}\int_0^1 B_2(x)\ln\sin(\pi x)\,dx &\approx 0.030\,448\,457\,058\,393,\\
  \frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi^2} &\approx 0.030\,448\,457\,058\,393.
\end{align*}
Agreement to within $10^{-17}$ in each case.
